# NFT binding standards: making items, progression and companion NFTs follow an immutable ERC-721 (Chog Genesis, Monad chain 143)

Research date: 2026-10-09. Target: Chog Genesis, `0xc96d31f8626c6d03fae5dcd3d61e3fb9f4a73763`, Monad mainnet (chain id 143), 1,969 tokens, metadata frozen.

Tag legend used on every claim:
- VERIFIED: read by me in the cited page or source file, or measured by me (JSON-RPC call, source code grep) on 2026-10-09.
- INFERRED: my reasoning from verified facts, OR a claim relayed by a search-engine summary of the cited URL that I did not open in full. The line says which.
- UNKNOWN: could not be established. What was tried is listed under Gaps.

ERC texts were read from the `ethereum/ERCs` repository at commit `ee5363be41aa55f2eb1199c693044c86dd464624` (committed 2026-10-08). Each `erc-N.md` file is the source of the rendered page `https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-N`.

Short answer to the team's question: yes, binding exists, in two families that both work on an unmodifiable ERC-721. Family A: the NFT controls a smart account that owns things (ERC-6551). Family B: a separate contract stores state or items keyed by `(chainId, collection, tokenId)` and reads `ownerOf` on the parent when needed. The nesting standards that put the logic inside the parent contract (ERC-7401, ERC-6059, ERC-6220, ERC-998 top-down, ERC-4907) cannot be applied to Chog Genesis.

## 1. ERC-6551 (token bound accounts): status, mechanics, pitfalls, cost, tooling

### Takeaway
ERC-6551 works today on Chog Genesis with zero change to the collection: the canonical registry and the tokenbound v0.3.1 account contracts are deployed on Monad mainnet with bytecode identical to Ethereum, and creating an account costs about 0.01 to 0.02 MON. The standard is still in Review (text unchanged since May 2024), the reference contracts have not moved since early 2024, and the well-known weakness is untouched: the seller controls the account until the sale settles and can empty it, and no mainstream marketplace was found that shows or protects the account contents.

Known pitfalls stated by the standard itself
- [VERIFIED] The ERC describes the seller-empties-the-account scam in its own Security Considerations: Alice deposits 10 ETH in the account, Bob offers 11 ETH for the token, "Alice withdraws 10ETH from the token bound account, and immediately accepts Bob's offer", "Bob receives token X, but account Y is empty". Source: [erc-6551.md, Fraud Prevention](https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/blob/master/ERCS/erc-6551.md)
- [VERIFIED] The ERC lists four mitigations and assigns them to marketplaces: attach the current account `state` to the order and void it if the state changed; attach asset commitments to the order; route the order through an external contract that performs these checks; implement a lock on the account. It then says: "Preventing fraud is outside the scope of this proposal." Source: [erc-6551.md, Fraud Prevention](https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/blob/master/ERCS/erc-6551.md)
- [VERIFIED] Ownership cycles: "All assets held in a token bound account may be rendered inaccessible if an ownership cycle is created"; cycles of depth greater than 1 are declared out of scope because on-chain prevention "is difficult to enforce". Source: [erc-6551.md, Ownership Cycles](https://github.com/ethereum/ERCs/blob/master/ERCS/erc-6551.md)
- [VERIFIED] A secondary explainer lists "Lingering Approvals": "When an NFT is sold, any token approvals set by the previous owner inside the TBA persist. A malicious seller could set an ERC-20 approval before selling the NFT, then drain newly deposited tokens using the lingering allowance." It also lists shared-implementation risk (one bug hits every proxy) and cross-chain limits ("If the TBA exists on a different chain than its bound NFT, the owner() function returns address(0), effectively disabling the account"). Source (secondary): [spark.money glossary, Tokenbound Account](https://www.spark.money/glossary/tokenbound-account)
- [INFERRED, search summary] In the ERC-6551 discussion thread, participants described a front-running variant (a transaction that removes the assets is ordered just before the atomic swap) and proposed a lockable account exposing a state fingerprint (ERC-5646 style) or a `lastTransactionTimestamp` that marketplaces would pin in the order. These are forum proposals, not part of the ERC. Source: [Ethereum Magicians, ERC-6551 thread](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/erc-6551-non-fungible-token-bound-accounts/13030)
- [INFERRED, search summary] No documented real-world incident and no formal audit of a "drain just before sale" attack surfaced in the searches; the evidence is the ERC's own scenario plus forum analysis. Source: search over [Ethereum Magicians thread](https://ethereum-magicians.org/t/erc-6551-non-fungible-token-bound-accounts/13030) and explainers such as [spark.money](https://www.spark.money/glossary/tokenbound-account)

### Inferences
- [INFERRED] "Follows the NFT" means exactly this: whoever `ownerOf(Chog)` returns at the moment of a call controls the account. A Seaport listing leaves the Chog in the seller's wallet until the order is filled, so the seller keeps full control of the account during the whole listing period. Nothing in ERC-6551 or in tokenbound v0.3.1 ties the account contents to the listing.
- [INFERRED] If a Chog is deposited in a staking contract, a vault or an escrow, that contract becomes the controller of the account, and since such a contract cannot call `execute`, the account is frozen for as long as the Chog stays there.
- [INFERRED] The `state()` pin and the `lock()` both leave one hole open: an asset that the account approved to a third-party operator earlier can be pulled out by that operator directly on the asset contract. That transfer never goes through the account, so `state()` does not change and the lock does not apply. A buyer or a marketplace must also check approvals, or the items must be non-transferable.
- [INFERRED] Because a lock persists across the transfer and cannot be shortened, a "lock, then list" flow protects the buyer only if the lock outlives the sale, and the buyer then inherits a locked account until expiry.
- [INFERRED] An account address is usable before the account exists (the six sampled Chog account addresses have no code, and the transfer simulation into one of them succeeded). A game can mint or send items to the counterfactual address and never pay for `createAccount`; the holder only needs to deploy the account the day they want to move something out.
- [INFERRED] If the items placed in the account are non-transferable (ERC-5192 or ERC-6454 style), the seller-empties-the-account problem disappears by construction and the account never needs to be deployed. The remaining benefit of ERC-6551 over a plain mapping is then only that items appear as standard token balances at a deterministic address that 6551-aware viewers understand.
- [INFERRED] Anyone can send anything to an account address (AccountV3 accepts ERC-721 and ERC-1155 from any contract). A seller can therefore stuff an account with worthless look-alike tokens to inflate the apparent value, and spam airdrops land there too. A buyer-facing view must filter by an allow-list of the game's own contracts.
- [INFERRED] Gas can be sponsored: AccountV3 is ERC-4337 compatible, the EntryPoint v0.6 it points to exists on Monad, and Monad lists a `Simple7702Account`. A live paymaster or bundler on Monad was not verified.
- [INFERRED] At the measured price, deploying and initialising an account for all 1,969 tokens would cost about 34 MON (1,969 x 171,403 gas x 102 gwei), under one US dollar at 0.0245 USD per MON. Cost is not the constraint; visibility and the sale-time race are.

## 2. Parent-child and composable standards: status, who must implement, adoption

### Takeaway
Every standard that makes the parent "own" children natively (ERC-7401, ERC-6059, ERC-6220, ERC-998 top-down) or adds a role to the parent (ERC-4907) must be implemented by the parent contract, and Chog Genesis answers `false` to all of their interface ids. The standards that put the logic in the child (ERC-998 bottom-up, ERC-5114, ERC-5700, ERC-7929) or in a registry (ERC-6551, ERC-7656) work with an immutable parent, but none of the child-side ones shows real adoption.

## 3. The simple pattern: companion contract keyed by the parent token id

### Takeaway
A contract that stores `state[tokenId]` (or mints items recorded against a parent token id) and checks `ChogGenesis.ownerOf(tokenId)` at use time is the oldest and most used way to bind something to a collection one does not control, and it is the only mechanism where the bound state cannot be stripped before a sale. Its price is that nothing shows it to a buyer unless the team builds that surface, and that any one-shot value keyed by token id is exposed to flash-loaned or borrowed ownership.

### Cited Findings
- [VERIFIED] The project's own contract analysis already records the finding for Chog Genesis: "No evolve, XP or level function exposed by the verified ChogMint implementation" and "Gameplay state can be kept externally under chain ID, collection address and canonical token ID without changing Genesis metadata." Source: local file `/Users/red.g/CascadeProjects/chogathon/research/evidence/genesis-mutability-facts.json`, explorer reference [monadscan.com](https://monadscan.com/address/0xc96d31f8626c6d03fae5dcd3d61e3fb9f4a73763#code)
- [VERIFIED] Axie Infinity splits progression in two: AXP is "an off-chain token" that is "gained by Axies through gameplay and other various Axie experiences", while levels are "on-chain numbers that are mapped to each Axie"; ascension is "a mechanism where users need to sign an on-chain transaction for an Axie to advance to certain levels" (levels 10, 20 and 30). Axie controls its own contracts, so this illustrates the keyed-state design, not the immutable-parent constraint. Source: [Sky Mavis docs, AXP overview](https://docs.skymavis.com/axie/axp/overview)
- [VERIFIED] Mocaverse: XP is per Moca NFT. ### Inferences
- [INFERRED] Advantages on an immutable parent: no dependency on any standard still in Review, no account to deploy, state cannot be removed by the seller (there is no transfer function to call), one cold read of `ownerOf` per use, and the team alone decides what is transferable. The state follows the token for free because it was never attached to a wallet.
- [INFERRED] Limits: no wallet or marketplace shows keyed state; other contracts cannot compose with it unless the team exposes a read interface; the state lives in a contract the team controls, so its durability equals the durability of that contract and of the team's keys.
- [INFERRED] Risk classes differ by what is keyed. Cumulative progression (level, specialisation unlocked) is safe against flash ownership because borrowing a Chog for one transaction gives nothing to extract. Anything claimable once per token (a reward, a free mint, a currency grant) repeats the ApeCoin and AGLD situation: it must be bound to holding duration, or be non-extractable, or the "already claimed" flag must be visible to buyers.
- [INFERRED] A fully off-chain variant (server database keyed by `(143, collection, tokenId)`, ownership checked by `ownerOf` at session start) has the same "follows the NFT" property with zero gas and zero on-chain proof. Axie's split (off-chain accrual, on-chain checkpoint at milestones) is the documented middle ground.
- [INFERRED] Estimated cost of one on-chain progression write on Monad, from the opcode table: base transaction, one cold call to Chog Genesis for `ownerOf` (10,100 account plus 8,100 storage page), one fresh-slot `SSTORE` (27,900 first time, 10,900 for a later overwrite on first access to its page), so roughly 50,000 to 80,000 gas, about 0.005 to 0.008 MON at 100 gwei. Derived from [docs.monad.xyz opcode-pricing](https://docs.monad.xyz/developer-essentials/opcode-pricing.md), not measured.
- [INFERRED] Because of the absence of ERC-4907 and of the delegate.xyz registry on Monad mainnet, a holder who keeps the Chog in a cold wallet, a vault or a staking contract cannot prove play rights from another address unless the game builds its own delegation.

- [UNKNOWN] A documented game that binds on-chain equipment or progression to a PFP collection it does not control, with usage numbers or a post-mortem. Not found. The closest sourced cases (Loot derivatives and AGLD, Book of Lore) are not games with a post-mortem.
- [UNKNOWN] Exact function-level behaviour of the AGLD claim contract and of the Book of Lore contract. The verified sources were not read on Etherscan.

## 4. Attestation-based binding (EAS or similar) on Monad

## 5. Real deployments and what happened

### Takeaway
The projects that made binding work in production control their own parent contract (Aavegotchi, Axie, Mocaverse) and pair the binding with their own marketplace surface and, for Aavegotchi, an on-chain lock at listing. The flagship ERC-6551 adopters either left no measurable outcome (Sapienz), moved away from NFT-held accounts (Lens V3), or shut down (Pirate Nation, for cost and player-count reasons unrelated to binding). No case was found of a game binding progression to a PFP collection it does not control with published results.

## 7. Game-design angle of binding

### Takeaway
Binding to the token turns play into resale value: the career, loadout and unlocks are sold with the hero. That is the selling point and also the source of the three known problems: a market for power (pay-to-win), an incentive to farm value with bots, and buyer deception at the sale. The teams that kept it under control limited what transfers, froze state at listing, or kept the fine-grained accrual off chain.

### Cited Findings
- [VERIFIED] The reference failure of tradable power in a loot game: Blizzard removed the Diablo III gold and real-money auction houses because the system "ultimately undermines Diablo's core game play: kill monsters to get cool loot", and "the shutdown will occur on March 18, 2014". The post is signed John Hight. Source: [Blizzard, Diablo III auction house update](https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/10974978/iii)
- [INFERRED, search summary] In the MOBA genre the dominant studio forbids the transfer of progression: Riot's support material says accounts are personal and non-transferable and that selling and buying them is strictly prohibited; an esports outlet reports nearly 300,000 League of Legends and Valorant accounts banned for rank manipulation and boosting (the start date of that enforcement wave differs across sources), and Riot's terms penalise brands that promote boosting or account sales. Sources: [Riot support, Account Sharing (Wild Rift)](https://support-wildrift.riotgames.com/hc/en-us/articles/360053284953-Account-Sharing), [Sheep Esports](https://www.sheepesports.com/de/articles/nearly-300-000-league-of-legends-and-valorant-accounts-banned-for-rank-manipulation/en)
- [VERIFIED] Axie Infinity keeps the grind off chain and commits only milestones: AXP is off-chain, levels are "on-chain numbers that are mapped to each Axie", and an on-chain transaction is required only to ascend at levels 10, 20 and 30. Source: [Sky Mavis docs, AXP overview](https://docs.skymavis.com/axie/axp/overview)
- [INFERRED, search summary] AXP is described as non-transferable and tied to each Axie, with a daily cap per Axie; sources disagree on the cap (4,500 per day in launch coverage, 2,000 on a later Sky Mavis page). Sources: [Sky Mavis Notion, What is AXP](https://skymavis.notion.site/AXP-Axie-Experience-Points-a8e5646abe1f49ae9d40dfdf429d9460), [eGamers](https://egamers.io/axie-infinity-launches-experience-points-and-leveling-system)

Source: [ERC721MarketplaceFacet.sol](https://github.com/aavegotchi/aavegotchi-contracts/blob/master/contracts/Aavegotchi/facets/ERC721MarketplaceFacet.sol), [AavegotchiGameFacet.sol](https://github.com/aavegotchi/aavegotchi-contracts/blob/master/contracts/Aavegotchi/facets/AavegotchiGameFacet.sol)

- [INFERRED, search summary] Pirate Nation's closure left players burning their on-chain items for certificates. Source: [BlockchainGamer](https://www.blockchaingamer.biz/news/40071/proof-of-play-shuts-down-pirate-nation-focus-pirate-token/)

### Inferences
- [INFERRED] What binding buys the player: the hero's resale price carries the career; a loadout and its unlocks need no re-setup after a purchase; a buyer can skip a grind; a seller can cash out time spent. What it buys the collection: a reason for the floor of played tokens to differ from unplayed ones.
- [INFERRED] In a MOBA, bound power that is purchasable is the exact thing the genre's incumbents ban (bought accounts, boosted ranks), because it puts a buyer in matches their skill does not justify. Binding cosmetic and identity progression (history, titles, specialisation choice) and binding matchmaking rating are different decisions; the second should stay with the player account, not with the token.
- [INFERRED] The botting incentive scales with how much resale value one hour of unattended play adds to a token. The sourced controls are a per-token daily cap (Axie), off-chain accrual with on-chain checkpoints only at milestones (Axie), and non-transferable base units (Pirate Nation Gen1, Pudgy World penguin).
- [INFERRED] The sale-time controls that have precedent are: freeze the bound state while listed (Aavegotchi), show the bound state on the listing (Mocaverse), and make bound value non-extractable so that there is nothing to strip (keyed state or soulbound items). ERC-6551 with transferable contents has none of the three by default.
- [INFERRED] Purchased specialisations that follow the token mean the buyer of a Chog gets what a previous holder paid for, and the seller loses it. Whether a purchase belongs to the token or to the buyer's wallet is a product decision with a direct revenue effect (a token-bound purchase is sold once per token, a wallet-bound one once per player).

## 8. Comparison table

### Takeaway
For an unmodifiable parent on Monad, three mechanisms are usable in a two-week build: keyed state in a companion contract (or off chain), ERC-6551 with the canonical contracts already on chain 143, and child-side binding of the game's own item contract. Everything that requires the parent to implement an interface is excluded, and nothing is visible on the one marketplace where the collection trades.

### Cited Findings

| Mechanism | Works with an immutable parent | On-chain footprint | Who pays gas | Visible on marketplaces | Complexity for a two-week build | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Off-chain state keyed by `(143, collection, tokenId)`, `ownerOf` checked at session start | Yes | None (one RPC read) | Nobody | No | Lowest | No public proof; state lives and dies with the game server |
| Companion contract storing progression and unlocks keyed by tokenId, `ownerOf` checked on write | Yes | One contract the team deploys; about 50,000 to 80,000 gas per write (estimated) | Player, or the team through a relayer or paymaster | No | Low | Team keys and upgrade policy; one-shot claims exposed to borrowed ownership |
| Hybrid: off-chain accrual, on-chain checkpoint at milestones (Axie model) | Yes | Same contract, far fewer writes | Player at milestones, or the team | No | Low to medium | Trust in the server between checkpoints |
| ERC-6551 account holding transferable item NFTs | Yes; registry, proxy and implementation verified on Monad | Item contracts; optional account deployment at 103,380 gas, or 171,403 with initialise (estimated) | Player (deploy, move items) or the team (mint to the counterfactual address) | No evidence of any marketplace display on Monad | Medium (SDK exists, last npm release 2024-10) | Seller empties the account before the sale; lingering approvals; ERC still in Review |

### Inferences
- [INFERRED] The gas estimates for companion-contract writes, the complexity column and the main-risk column are my assessment, not sourced measurements.
- [INFERRED] The three requirements of the team map to different mechanisms. Game progression and purchased specialisations are state that must not be strippable: keyed state (companion contract, off chain, or the hybrid) gives that by construction. Companion NFTs that should be separately tradable are the one case where ERC-6551 adds something (standard ownership at an address controlled by the Chog), at the price of the sale-time race. Companion NFTs that should never leave the hero need no account at all: a parent pointer in the companion contract is enough.
- [INFERRED] The mechanisms combine. A companion contract can be the source of truth today, and items can later be minted to the ERC-6551 address of each Chog without migrating anything, because that address is fixed by `(registry, implementation, salt, 143, collection, tokenId)` and already computable.
- [INFERRED] Whatever is chosen, the deliverable that makes "follows it everywhere" real for a buyer is a public per-token page, since no marketplace surface exists for it on Monad.
