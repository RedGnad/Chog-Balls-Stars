# Hero kit design and balance for a trait-derived roster (research notes, as of 2026-10-09)

Scope: reference literature and production practice for a 3v3 top-down, short-session mobile MOBA/brawler in which each of 1,969 NFTs derives its class and abilities from fixed visible traits.

Conventions used in every finding:
- Tag 1, confidence: VERIFIED (the cited page or record was fetched in this session and says this), INFERRED (my reasoning or arithmetic from verified items, or general knowledge not re-checked today), UNKNOWN (looked for, not found or not confirmable).
- Tag 2, source type: primary (developer, publisher, author, official data feed), secondary (press, third-party guide), wiki (community wiki), excerpt (seen only as a search-engine excerpt, page itself not opened).
- Tag 3, nature: principle (a published design rule), measured (a number or observed fact), opinion (a stated view, including one studio's choice).
- One studio's choice is never a universal law. Where a claim is a single studio's practice it is tagged opinion or says so.

Source access notes (what failed, what worked):
- Liquipedia, Brawlify API, Fandom HTML pages, Bulbapedia, DappRadar, The Defiant, TouchArcade, BusinessWire: HTTP 403 even with a browser User-Agent. Workaround that worked for Fandom wikis: the MediaWiki API (`https://<wiki>.fandom.com/api.php?action=parse&page=<Page>&prop=wikitext&format=json` and `action=query&prop=categoryinfo`).
- `nexus.leagueoflegends.com` now redirects to the League home page. Workaround: Wayback Machine (`https://web.archive.org/web/2019/<url>`).

## 1. Class and role taxonomies used by leading action team games

### Takeaway
Shipped team action games converge on 5 to 7 mechanically defined classes (Brawl Stars 7, League 7 with 11 subclasses, Heroes of the Storm 6, Mobile Legends 6, Pokemon UNITE 5, Smite 5), and two studios (Riot, Blizzard) publicly split coarser schemes because 4 to 6 broad labels stopped describing what a kit does. Three broad categories exist only as a top layer (Battlerite: melee, ranged, support).

### Cited Findings
- [VERIFIED, primary via quoted repost, principle] Riot's 2016 dev blog "Classes & Subclasses" (James "Statikk" Bach) groups champions by in-game effect, not theme, to create "a shared vocabulary", and calls the scheme "more of a set of guidelines than rigid rules". Classes and subclasses then: Tanks (Vanguards, Wardens), Fighters (Juggernauts, Divers), Slayers (Assassins, Skirmishers), Mages (Burst, Battle, Artillery), Controllers (Enchanters, Disruptors), Marksmen (no subclass), plus hybrids and "distinct playstyles" kept outside the grid. Source: [Surrender at 20 red post collection quoting the blog](https://www.surrenderat20.net/2016/04/red-post-collection-dev-blog-on-classes.html) (original URL given there: `http://na.leagueoflegends.com/en/news/game-updates/gameplay/dev-blog-classes-subclasses`, not reachable today).
- [VERIFIED, wiki hosted on Riot's domain, measured] Current League scheme: 7 classes (Controller, Fighter, Mage, Marksman, Slayer, Tank, Specialist) and 11 subclasses (Enchanter, Catcher, Juggernaut, Diver, Burst, Battlemage, Artillery, Assassin, Skirmisher, Vanguard, Warden). The page dates the final draft of the rework to May 24, 2017 and notes that the game client still filters by the older primary tags. Source: [League of Legends Wiki, Champion classes](https://wiki.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/Champion_classes).
- [VERIFIED, primary, principle, paraphrased by the fetch tool] Supercell's April 3, 2023 patch notes define 7 Brawl Stars classes and publish their counter relations. Damage Dealer: sustained or burst damage, well rounded. Assassin: closes distance, high burst, low HP. Marksman (new): high damage at long range, single-shot, slow reload, low HP. Artillery (new): throws over walls, low HP, no burst. Tank: high HP or mitigation, close range. Support: heals or buffs allies, low burst. Controller (new): area control with area abilities, fair HP. Published counters: Damage Dealers beat Tanks and Assassins and lose to Marksmen and Artillery; Assassins beat Marksmen and Artillery and are hard-countered by Tanks; Tanks counter Assassins and are susceptible to Damage Dealers; Artillery struggle against Tanks and Assassins. Source: [#MysteryAtTheHub Patch Notes](https://supercell.com/en/games/brawlstars/blog/release-notes/mysteryatthehub-patch-notes/).
- [VERIFIED, wiki, measured on 2026-10-09] Brawl Stars class distribution by wiki category count: Damage Dealer 23, Controller 21, Assassin 19, Tank 16, Support 13, Marksman 11, Artillery 7. Category "Brawlers" holds 110 pages and the wiki computes the roster as pages minus 1, so about 109 brawlers. Rarity categories: Rare 8, Super Rare 10, Epic 30, Mythic 43, Legendary 14, Ultra 2, Starting 1. Source: [Brawl Stars Wiki API, category info](https://brawlstars.fandom.com/api.php?action=query&prop=categoryinfo&titles=Category:Brawlers&format=json) (same call with `Category:Damage_Dealer_Brawlers`, etc.).
- [VERIFIED, primary, measured] Brawl Stars kit structure in 2026, as printed for new brawlers: optional Trait, Attack, Super, two Gadgets, two Star Powers, one Hypercharge. Example: Damian (Mythic, Tank), Bolt (Epic, Tank), Starr Nova (Mythic, Assassin). Source: [Release Notes April 2026](https://supercell.com/en/games/brawlstars/blog/release-notes/release-notes-april-2026/).
- [VERIFIED, primary, measured] "Buffies" add a further layer: "Each Brawler gets 3 Buffies, one for both Starr Powers, one for both Gadgets, and another one for the Hypercharge", bought with 1000 Coins and 2000 Power Points from a Claw Machine; Epic and Mythic Gears are removed for brawlers that receive Buffies. Source: [Supercell, new power, Brawl Pass changes and a new Starr Drop](https://supercell.com/en/games/brawlstars/blog/news/new-power-brawl-pass-changes-and-a-new-starr-drop-2).
- [VERIFIED, secondary, opinion] Wikipedia states Brawl Stars rarities "vary based on a Brawler's concept difficulty". No Supercell statement confirming that rarity tracks complexity and not power was found. Source: [Wikipedia, Brawl Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brawl_Stars).
- [VERIFIED, primary, principle] Blizzard replaced the four Heroes of the Storm roles with six (Tank, Bruiser, Support, Healer, Melee Assassin, Ranged Assassin) because "the existing roles have grown limited in accurately describing each individual hero" and "to give both new and existing players a clearer idea of what kind of kit to expect during hero select". Source: [Blizzard, Hero Role Expansion](https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/22921910/novye-roli-geroev).
- [VERIFIED, primary, measured] Pokemon UNITE uses 5 battle types, each defined by a stat profile: Attacker ("low Endurance and excel at dealing heavy ranged damage"), Speedster ("high Mobility and Offense"), All-Rounder ("balanced Offense and Endurance"), Defender ("high Endurance and excel at protecting allies and hindering opponents"), Supporter ("inflicting status conditions on opponents and by healing allies"). Source: [Pokemon UNITE official roster page](https://unite.pokemon.com/en-us/pokemon/).
- [VERIFIED, primary data feed, measured on 2026-10-09] Dota 2 lists 127 heroes. Each hero carries one of 4 primary attributes (counts 36, 35, 34, 22), a 9-slot `role_levels` vector graded 0 to 3 (a hero has several roles at different strengths), and an official complexity rating of 1 to 3 (55, 59 and 13 heroes). Source: [Dota 2 data feed, hero list](https://www.dota2.com/datafeed/herolist?language=english), [hero data example](https://www.dota2.com/datafeed/herodata?language=english&hero_id=1).
- [VERIFIED, secondary, measured] Mobile Legends: 6 roles (Tank, Marksman, Assassin, Fighter, Mage, Support), 133 heroes as of June 17, 2026. Smite: 5 classes (Assassin, Guardian, Hunter, Mage, Warrior), 130 gods. Sources: [Wikipedia, Mobile Legends: Bang Bang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_Legends:_Bang_Bang), [Wikipedia, Smite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smite_(video_game)).
- [VERIFIED, primary, measured] Battlerite: champions "specialise in either melee, support, or ranged combat" (3 categories), played 2v2 and 3v3. Source: [Stunlock Studios press release, Nov 8, 2017](https://press.stunlock.com/freetoplay/).
- [VERIFIED, secondary, measured] Omega Strikers: three-on-three, two positions (forward, goalie), usually two forwards and one goalie. Source: [Wikipedia, Omega Strikers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omega_Strikers) and search excerpts of player guides.
- [VERIFIED, primary, measured] Squad Busters: Supercell ended active development on October 30, 2025, final update December 2025, closure expected "not before the middle of the year" 2026. Source: [Supercell, Squad Busters development ending](https://supercell.com/en/news/squad-development-ending/).

### Inferences
- [INFERRED] Readability comes from three recurring devices, not from the class count alone: (a) each class is a stat envelope plus a job (Supercell, Pokemon UNITE definitions), (b) published counter relations (Supercell 2023), (c) an explicit "does not fit" bucket (Riot's Specialist, Riot's hybrids) so the grid is not bent to fit outliers.
- [INFERRED] Dota 2 shows an alternative to a single class label: a graded role vector plus an official complexity rating. This suits a trait-derived roster, where a kit can be "Controller 2, Support 1".
- [INFERRED] The team's draft of 3 classes sits below every shipped scheme above except Battlerite's top layer. With 1,969 units, 3 labels will each cover hundreds of different kits and will not tell an opponent what to expect.

### Gaps
- Riot's 2017 follow-up post ("Taking another look at subclasses") could not be fetched; the 2017 changes are taken from the wiki.
- Wild Rift role list: the official champions page is client rendered, not verified. Squad Busters and Omega Strikers class definitions from official pages: not found.
- Dota 2 role names for the 9 slots are not in the data feed (commonly Carry, Support, Nuker, Disabler, Jungler, Durable, Escape, Pusher, Initiator: INFERRED, not re-verified).

Verified launch rosters range from about 10 to 40 characters, with 15 to 25 typical for small or mobile-first teams; only Dota 2, a port of an existing game, launched with about 100. Publishers do not publish "distinct ability" counts, so those are arithmetic from kit structure.

### Inferences
- [INFERRED, arithmetic] Authored ability counts at launch, from roster times kit size: Battlerite about 22 x 7 = 154; League about 40 x 5 = 200 (passive plus four, kit size from general knowledge); Brawl Stars today about 109 x 7 = 763 authored pieces. The two day counts for the Brawl Stars soft launch are consistent: 522 runs to the interview date, 544 to the global launch.
- [INFERRED] The team's draft needs 19 authored building blocks (5 shapes, 10 aura values including "none", 4 deliveries) plus 3 class envelopes, yet yields 5 x 10 x 4 = 200 ability variants and 600 kits with class. That is the same order of magnitude of distinct abilities as a 20 to 40 hero launch roster, for far less authoring, provided every block is visually distinct.

## 3. Combinatorial and modular ability systems that scale without losing coherence

### Takeaway
Systems that combine parts stay predictable when they enforce a small set of hard rules: one modifier per slot, a fixed precedence or reaction table, duplicates do not stack, each "element" owns one signature effect, and complexity is rationed by tier. The team's shape x effect x delivery draft is structurally the same as Hades (slot x god) and Magicka (element x cast mode).

### Cited Findings
- [VERIFIED, wiki, measured] Hades: each Olympian owns a signature effect (Aphrodite Weak, Ares Doom, Artemis Critical, Athena Deflect, Demeter Chill, Dionysus Hangover, Poseidon Knockback, Zeus Chain lightning). "Zagreus' Attack, Special, Cast, Dash, and Call abilities have one boon slot, and cannot hold multiple ability boons"; a new god's boon replaces the old one. Eight gods times five slots is the core grid. Boon rarity (Common, Rare, Epic, Heroic) means "the same numbers get bigger". Source: [Hades Wiki, Boons](https://hades.fandom.com/wiki/Boons).
- [VERIFIED, secondary excerpt, opinion] Greg Kasavin named Dota 2 as a "big reference point" for Hades ability and boon design, and described multiplicative content as central to replayability. Source: [AFK Gaming](https://afkgaming.com/dota2/news/6052-hades-dev-shares-how-dota-2-influenced-their-game) (page not opened, excerpt only).
- [VERIFIED, secondary, measured] Magicka: eight base elements, up to five chained per spell, opposing elements cannot be combined (fire and cold), and "there is a set hierarchy which determines the type of the spell cast" (shield over projectile over beam over steam over lightning). "The order in which the component elements of a spell are summoned has no effect". Each spell can be cast in four ways (projectile or beam, area, weapon enhancement, self). Source: [Wikipedia, Magicka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magicka).
- [VERIFIED, wiki, measured] Genshin Impact: 7 elements; applying an element leaves an aura that decays; a second element triggers a reaction from a fixed table and consumes the aura (Vaporize 1.5x or 2x depending on order, Melt 2x); elemental application has an internal cooldown so not every hit reacts. Source: [Genshin Impact Wiki, Elemental Reaction](https://genshin-impact.fandom.com/wiki/Elemental_Reaction).
- [VERIFIED, wiki, measured] Path of Exile: support gems "modify any active skill gems in sockets linked to the support gem, while also typically increasing the mana cost of the skill" (power has a cost); "if two copies of a support gem are linked to the same skill, only the higher level gem will be used" (no duplicate stacking); some combinations leave the skill unmodified. Source: [Path of Exile Wiki, Support gem](https://pathofexile.fandom.com/wiki/Support_gem).
- [VERIFIED, primary, principle] Magic: The Gathering "New World Order" (Mark Rosewater, Dec 5, 2011) separates three complexity types: comprehension complexity ("can the players read the card and know how it works"), board complexity (how cards interact on the battlefield), strategic complexity. "Comprehension complexity and board complexity are a problem for beginners. Strategic complexity is not. Why? Because beginners can't see strategic complexity." Rule adopted: "If a common card is complex, it has to have a reason"; complexity is pushed to higher rarities. "Just one card ... can change the design tree from a few choices to a double-digit number of choices." Reported result: "New World Order brought clarity to the game." Source: [Wizards of the Coast, New World Order](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/new-world-order-2011-12-05).
- [VERIFIED, wiki, principle] Slay the Spire defines a keyword as "a word or term that has a specific, predefined meaning within the game's mechanics", shown in bold; debuffs such as Poison have one fixed rule each. Source: [Slay the Spire Wiki, Keywords](https://slay-the-spire.fandom.com/wiki/Keywords).
- [VERIFIED, wiki on Riot's domain, measured] League of Legends crowd control taxonomy: 18 named types (Airborne, Blind, Cripple, Disarm, Disrupt, Forced action, Ground, Kinematics, Knockdown, Nearsight, Silence, Drowsy and Sleep, Slow, Root, Stasis, Stun, Suppression). A single table states for each type whether movement, attacking and abilities are disabled, whether it interrupts channels, whether Tenacity reduces it and whether cleanses remove it. Airborne and Suppression are not reduced by Tenacity. Source: [League of Legends Wiki, Types of Crowd Control](https://wiki.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/Types_of_Crowd_Control).
- [VERIFIED, wiki plus secondary, measured] World of Warcraft diminishing returns: repeated crowd control of the same category on the same target within a window is shortened (full, then half, then further reduced or immune). A Blizzard hotfix in patch 6.1 fixed the window at exactly 18 seconds; third-party guides report later changes (16 seconds and a chain limit of 2 in Midnight). Sources: [Warcraft Wiki, Diminishing returns](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Diminishing_returns), [Icy Veins on the 6.1 hotfix](https://icy-veins.com/forums/topic/11168-patch-61-crowd-control-diminishing-return-hotfix), [Maxroll](https://maxroll.gg/wow/resources/crowd-control-diminishing-returns).
- [VERIFIED, primary, measured] Brawl Stars treats stacking slows as a bug: "Squeak's Residue slow could stack and immobilize players", fixed in the February to March 2026 notes. Source: [Release Notes February 2026](https://supercell.com/en/games/brawlstars/blog/release-notes/release-notes-february-2026/).

### Inferences
- [INFERRED] Rules that recur across these systems and make combinations predictable: (1) one modifier per axis or slot, replacement instead of accumulation (Hades, Path of Exile); (2) a published resolution rule when parts conflict (Magicka precedence, Genshin reaction table); (3) each source owns exactly one signature effect with one name and one visual (Hades gods, Slay the Spire keywords); (4) same-effect reapplication refreshes or is ignored, it never multiplies (Path of Exile duplicates, Brawl Stars slow fix, WoW diminishing returns); (5) the tier axis rations complexity (Magic) or scales numbers inside a single-player run (Hades), and neither case is a persistent PvP power advantage.
- [INFERRED] The draft already satisfies rule 1 (one effect per axis). It has no stated answer yet for rule 2 (what happens when a burn and a slow from two players land on one target, or when a heal aura meets a poison) and rule 4 (stacking table).

### Gaps
- Pokemon type chart numbers (18 types and the 0, 0.5, 1, 2 multipliers) were not re-verified today (Bulbapedia 403): INFERRED from general knowledge.
- Hearthstone keyword policy and Magic's evergreen keyword list: not researched in this session.
- No primary Supergiant text on the boon grid design was fetched; the Hades structure comes from the community wiki.

## 4. NFT or trait-driven games that derived gameplay from traits

### Takeaway
The documented pattern is consistent: when rarity or scarce parts carry stats, entry cost and pay-to-win pressure follow, and teams later add free baseline units, hide stats, or rework the system. The cleanest positive pattern found is Aavegotchi's two-sided trait map, where both ends of a trait give a benefit and a cost.

### Cited Findings
- [VERIFIED, primary, measured] Axie Infinity Classic (December 2019): "Axies draw cards based on the body parts they possess." Origins (early access April 6, 2022) "introduces a real-time combat system, 3 free starter Axies, and vertical progression through Rune & Charm crafting"; "This is the first time that eyes & ears have ever had cards!"; critical hits replaced by a Rage mechanic. Source: [Axie Infinity whitepaper, Battling](https://whitepaper.axieinfinity.com/gameplay/battling).
- [VERIFIED, primary, opinion] Sky Mavis's stated reasons for Origin (Feb 11, 2022): random critical hits "often over influences matches"; the game was "built from the ground up to be more approachable for a mainstream audience"; starter Axies are free, non-NFT and cannot earn SLP; all six parts now give cards (four in V2); runes and charms are seasonal and removed each season. Holders were told "You will be able to use the Axies you own now in Origin" and that alpha progress would be reset. Source: [Axie Infinity: Origin is Coming!](https://blog.axieinfinity.com/p/origin).
- [VERIFIED, secondary, measured] Axie Classic class triangle: class advantage gives 15% more damage, disadvantage 15% less, and a card matching the Axie's own class gets a 10% bonus. Entry cost in late 2021: "the cheapest playable set of axies costs around $600-$800", which created the scholarship lending market. Sources: [AFK Gaming, class triangle](https://afkgaming.com/blockchaingames/guide/axie-infinity-triangle-explained-what-are-classes-and-how-do-they-work), [Metaguild scholarship page](https://metaguild.notion.site/2-Scholarship-Program-3175118028c142c9a2223371d878e4c3) (both read as search excerpts).
- [VERIFIED, primary, measured] Aavegotchi: six traits, four of which (NRG, AGG, SPK, BRN) affect gameplay. Values are "distributed on a bell-curve": Common 25-74 (50%), Uncommon 10-24 and 75-90 (30%), Rare 2-9 and 91-97 (16%), Mythical 0-1 and 98-99 (4%). Rarity is distance from the middle, at either end. Base Rarity Score also "is used to boost a gotchi's carrying capacity" (BRS 600 gives 400, BRS 900 gives 900). Source: [Aavegotchi Wiki, Traits](https://wiki.aavegotchi.com/en/traits).
- [VERIFIED, primary, principle] Aavegotchi Gotchiverse trait map (version 2): every trait is a trade. Low NRG: plus HP capacity, minus AP capacity; high NRG: the reverse. Low AGG: plus Defense Power, minus Action Speed; high AGG: the reverse. Low SPK: plus Regen, minus Ethereality; high SPK: the reverse. Low BRN: plus Melee Power, minus Ranged Power; high BRN: the reverse. Two scaling models were still open ("Yin/Yang" versus "Buff Only") and "The AavegotchiDAO proposed and voted for an alternative Gotchiverse Trait Map". Source: [Aavegotchi Wiki, Trait Mappings](https://wiki.aavegotchi.com/en/trait-mappings).
- [VERIFIED, primary, measured] DeFi Kingdoms ties rarity to power: "Rare Heroes are coveted because each level of rarity corresponds with bonuses to the base stat levels of your Hero" (Common, Uncommon, Rare, Legendary, Mythic); starting stats are "determined by their class, their stat bonus genes, their rarity". Sources: [DFK docs, Rarity](https://docs.defikingdoms.com/gameplay/heroes/rarity), [DFK docs, Stats](https://docs.defikingdoms.com/gameplay/heroes/stats).
- [VERIFIED, primary, opinion] Galaxy Fight Club (cross-collection PFP brawler, whitepaper last updated about four years ago): a player with no avatar gets "a default fighter with base stats and a default base tier weapon"; partner-collection avatars are playable; "The only difference is that the loot/rewards that you can earn from winning are different based on the avatar or weapon you play with". Weapons "make up 50% of the strength of a fighter" and "the strength of the weapon is correlated with their rarity and tier" (8 tiers). Battle stats are deliberately hidden: "part of the enjoyment is figuring out which fighter or which traits are stronger, secondly we do not want this to turn into a pay-to-win game". Sources: [GFC whitepaper, Participation](https://whitepaper.galaxyfightclub.com/gameplay/participation), [Fighters](https://whitepaper.galaxyfightclub.com/economy/fighters), [Weapons](https://whitepaper.galaxyfightclub.com/economy/weapons).
- [VERIFIED, primary, measured] Loot Survivor derives combat from item type with an intransitive rule: "Weapon triangle system (Magical > Brute > Hunter > Magical)"; weapon versus armor chart (Blade strong against Cloth, weak against Metal; Bludgeon strong against Hide, weak against Cloth; Magic strong against Metal, weak against Hide); items run from T1 (Legendary) to T5 (Common) and gain "greatness". Sources: [Loot Survivor docs](https://docs.provable.games/lootsurvivor), [BibliothecaDAO README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BibliothecaDAO/loot-survivor/main/README.md).
- [VERIFIED, publisher store listing and wiki, measured] Illuvium: every Illuvial has one class and one affinity, which drive team synergies; evolution adds a second class and affinity (composite synergies). Sources: [Epic Games Store listing](https://store.epicgames.com/p/illuvium-60064c), [Liquipedia beginner guide](https://liquipedia.net/illuvium/Beginners_Guide) (both as search excerpts).
- [VERIFIED, secondary, measured] Pixelmon: February 2022 mint at 3 ETH each, about $70 million raised, buyers purchased sight unseen; the reveal showed unfinished art. The founder wrote "we made a horrible mistake" and pledged $2,000,000 to redo the art. Source: [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/110542/nft-game-pixelmon-attempts-comeback-after-70m-horrible-art-reveal).
- [VERIFIED, secondary, measured] Thetan Arena: each hero has a hero rarity and a skin rarity that determine rewards; free starter heroes cannot earn tokens or be traded. Source: [prom.io Thetan Arena FAQ](https://prom.io/games/thetanarena/faq) (excerpt).
- [VERIFIED, primary, opinion] A non-crypto parallel on holder sensitivity to power items: Supercell introduced Buffies with "We understand that progression can be a sensitive topic" and "We want to focus more on utility and skill expression as opposed to raw strength (like Hypercharge was)". ## 5. Balance methodology a small team can apply

### Takeaway
The applicable toolkit is: one cost curve that prices every building block against a common budget, an explicit intransitive counter structure between classes, numeric bands that trigger a change, and a rule that weak is safer than strong. Published thresholds exist only from Riot.

### Cited Findings
- [VERIFIED, primary, principle] Schreiber, Game Balance Concepts, Level 3 (July 21, 2010): in transitive systems stronger things must cost more; "We want the costs and benefits to be equal, numerically"; every effect is converted into one resource so different effects compare directly. Method: price single-purpose effects first, then combinations, isolate unknowns by comparing objects that differ by one feature. "It's better to make an object too weak than to make it too strong." A choice between benefits costs at least as much as the more expensive option. Source: [Game Balance Concepts, Level 3](https://gamebalanceconcepts.wordpress.com/2010/07/21/level-3-transitive-mechanics-and-cost-curves/).
- [VERIFIED, primary, principle] Schreiber, Level 9 (Sept 1, 2010): intransitive mechanics are "games where there is no single dominant strategy, because everything can be beaten by something else"; they act as an emergency brake, "not a substitute for good balance". Build the payoff table, remove dominated options, solve for the mixed-strategy ratio, then compare with observed pick rates. Worked result: if Rock wins count double, Paper is played half the time. Source: [Game Balance Concepts, Level 9](https://gamebalanceconcepts.wordpress.com/2010/09/01/level-9-intransitive-mechanics/).
- [VERIFIED, primary record, measured] Book form: Ian Schreiber and Brenda Romero, "Game Balance", CRC Press, 2021, ISBN 9781315156422; chapter 1 "The Foundations of Game Balance", pp. 3-24. Source: [Crossref record, DOI 10.1201/9781315156422-1](https://doi.org/10.1201/9781315156422-1). The book text itself was not consulted.
- [VERIFIED, primary, measured] Riot's Champion Balance Framework (May 30, 2019): four audiences. Average play (below the top 10%): overpowered above 54.5% win rate if ban rate is below average, sliding to 52.5% at five times the average ban rate (about 7% then); underpowered below 49%. Skilled play (top 10%): 54% sliding to 52%; floor 49%. Elite (top 0.1%): 45% ban rate; floor 5% presence. Pro: 90% presence on one patch or 80% across consecutive patches; floor 5%. A champion is overpowered if it crosses the top end for any audience, underpowered only if below the floor for all. Stated aim: limit subjective judgment. Source: [/dev: Champion Balance Framework](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/dev-champion-balance-framework/).
- [VERIFIED, primary, measured] Riot's 2020 update: "We tightened the OP bands for both Average and Skilled play by 0.5% each", widened Elite to the top 0.5% and loosened Pro bands. Source: [/dev: Balance Framework Update](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/dev-balance-framework-update/).
- [VERIFIED, primary, opinion, paraphrased by the fetch tool] Supercell's April 2026 balance notes justify changes with usage and win rate, without publishing thresholds: Grom nerfed for "climbing in usage and win rate", BiBi for the highest upper-level win rate, Leon as "consistently top-performing" with the aim of a glass-cannon feel, Fang buffed because "too feast-or-famine", Glowy buffed because earlier changes made him "too niche". Source: [Release Notes April 2026](https://supercell.com/en/games/brawlstars/blog/release-notes/release-notes-april-2026/).
- [VERIFIED, primary excerpt, opinion] Supercell on Clash Royale: balance combines playtesting with "looking at the stats, particularly card use rates and win rates", with monthly changes. A third-party site reports an acceptable win-rate range of 45 to 55%, not confirmed by Supercell. Sources: [Supercell, Clash Royale balance post](https://supercell.com/en/games/clashroyale/blog/release-notes/balance-changes-coming-1), [RoyaleAPI](https://royaleapi.com/blog/champion-card-trends), [GDC 2017, Quest for the Healthy Metagame (Stefan Engblom)](https://gdcvault.com/play/1024272/Quest-for-the-Healthy-Metagame) (talk is members-only).
- [VERIFIED, primary abstract, principle] Tom Cadwell (Riot), GDC 2013, "Counterplay and Teamplay in Multiplayer Game Design": multiplayer design needs "counterplay (gameplay for the opponents of a player) and teamplay (gameplay for the teammates of a player)". Source: [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1018158/Counterplay-and-Teamplay-in-Multiplayer).
- [VERIFIED, repost of a 2010 Riot forum post, opinion] Cadwell's ("Zileas") anti-pattern list: Power Without Gameplay ("a big benefit in a way that players don't find satisfying or don't notice", example team auras, which then need overbuffing), Burden of Knowledge (works "ONLY IF the victim understands what is going on"), Unclear Optimization, Fun Fails to Exceed Anti-Fun ("the 'anti-fun' created on your use of a mechanic is greater than your fun"), Conflicted Purpose, Anti-Combo, False Choice, Non-Reliability ("When a skill is highly unreliable, we have to overpower it to make it 'satisfying enough'"). Source: [Law of Game Design repost](https://lawofgamedesign.com/2014/02/20/zileas-list-of-game-design-anti-fun-patterns/).
- [VERIFIED, primary via Wayback, principle] Riot's VFX style guide (Riot Jino, Oct 25, 2017): "VFX should communicate a spell's gameplay space, power, and function, without creating a distraction"; "visual impact should represent gameplay impact". Four goals: "Provide visual clarity for gameplay; Minimize visual clutter; Promote a champion's theme; Surprise and delight players", achieved through "gameplay, value, color, shape, and timing". Source: [/dev: League's VFX Style Guide, archived](https://web.archive.org/web/20191022110249/https://nexus.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/2017/10/dev-leagues-vfx-style-guide/).
- [VERIFIED, secondary excerpt of the Riot PDF, principle] The guide separates a primary element (the readable gameplay shape: high value contrast, clear silhouette, high opacity) from secondary elements (theme: low opacity, subtle), and requires the drawn area to match the real area of effect; Riot's later VFX update post ranks fixing inaccurate hitbox indicators first. Sources: [Riot VFX style guide PDF](https://nexus.leagueoflegends.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/VFX_Styleguide_final_public_hidpjqwx7lqyx0pjj3ss.pdf), [Riot, behind the scenes of VFX updates](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-au/news/dev/dev-behind-the-scenes-of-vfx-updates/) (both via search excerpts).
- [VERIFIED, secondary excerpt, opinion] Valve on Team Fortress 2 unlockables (Robin Walker): "We want him to give up some of his primary advantage in return for something else"; an unlockable can act as a subclass, and a new class is "an order of magnitude more work than adding a new unlockable". Source: [Shacknews, Valve talks Sniper unlockable](https://www.shacknews.com/article/58501/valve-talks-team-fortress-2).
- [VERIFIED title only, opinion] Extra Credits, "Perfect Imbalance - Why Unbalanced Design Creates Balanced Play": argues for deliberate, controlled imbalance to keep a metagame moving. Content summarised from forum discussions, not from a transcript. Source: [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e31OSVZF77w).
- [VERIFIED, secondary, measured] Slay the Spire (Anthony Giovannetti, GDC 2019): the team logged every playtest run to a metrics server and tracked pick rate per card and presence in winning decks to find underpowered and overpowered cards, and used data to filter community complaints. Sources: [GDC Vault listing](https://www.gdcvault.com/browse/gdc-19/play/1025731), [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/learn-i-slay-the-spire-i-s-metrics-driven-approach-to-game-balancing-at-gdc-2019).

### Inferences
- [INFERRED] After the 2020 tightening, Riot's Average band would read 54% to 52% and Skilled 53.5% to 51.5% (arithmetic from the stated 0.5%; the updated numbers are only in an image).
- [INFERRED] For a trait-derived roster the cost curve is the central tool: price each shape, each aura effect and each delivery mode in one currency (for example expected damage-equivalent per second of cooldown), then set the kit budget equal for all 1,969 units. "Sidegrade" is then a checkable property: equal budget, different spend.
- [INFERRED] Riot's rule "overpowered if it crosses the top for any audience, underpowered only if below the floor for all" translates to bots as: flag a building block if any bot persona over-performs with it; buff only if every persona under-performs.

### Gaps
- Supercell has not published numeric win-rate or use-rate thresholds for Brawl Stars: not found.
- Schell ("The Art of Game Design"), Sylvester ("Designing Games") and Burgun were not consulted: no accessible full text in this session, so nothing is attributed to them.
- No GDC talk on Battlerite telegraphing was found. A Brawl Stars GDC talk on combat readability was not found either (the GDC 2019 talk covers controls and progression).

## 6. Automated and simulation-based balancing

### Takeaway
The literature supports a concrete loop for a game with a deterministic simulation and bots: measure each feature by restricting it, rate kits from a win matrix with a model that tolerates intransitivity, and search parameters toward a designer-specified target matchup graph. Two cautions recur: bots are not players, and a balanced number is not the same as a level or kit players perceive as good.

### Cited Findings
- [VERIFIED, primary record] Jaffe, A., Miller, A., Andersen, E., Liu, Y.-E., Karlin, A., Popovic, Z. (2012). "Evaluating Competitive Game Balance with Restricted Play." Proc. AAAI Conf. on Artificial Intelligence and Interactive Digital Entertainment, 8(1), 26-31. Formulation: "carefully restricted agents are played against standard agents"; the win rate of an agent forbidden from using a feature measures that feature's importance. Tool applied to an educational card game. [DOI 10.1609/aiide.v8i1.12513](https://doi.org/10.1609/aiide.v8i1.12513).
- [VERIFIED, primary record] Volz, V., Rudolph, G., Naujoks, B. (2016). "Demonstrating the Feasibility of Automatic Game Balancing." GECCO 2016, pp. 269-276. Multi-objective evolutionary search over Top Trumps decks with simulation-based fitness (fairness and excitement, e.g. win rate and number of tricks); decks at least as good as published ones; concludes a multi-objective approach is necessary. [DOI 10.1145/2908812.2908913](https://doi.org/10.1145/2908812.2908913), [arXiv:1603.03795](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.03795).
- [VERIFIED, primary record] de Mesentier Silva, F., Lee, S., Togelius, J., Nealen, A. (2017). "AI-based Playtesting of Contemporary Board Games." FDG 2017, pp. 1-10. Four agents with different play styles on Ticket to Ride; style effectiveness depends on map and player count; agents also found states not covered by the rules. [DOI 10.1145/3102071.3102105](https://doi.org/10.1145/3102071.3102105).
- [VERIFIED, primary record] de Mesentier Silva, F., Canaan, R., Lee, S., Fontaine, M. C., Togelius, J., Hoover, A. K. (2019). "Evolving the Hearthstone Meta." IEEE CoG 2019. Evolutionary search over card attribute changes so that deck matchups approach 50%, then a multi-objective version that also minimises the number of changes. [DOI 10.1109/CIG.2019.8847966](https://doi.org/10.1109/CIG.2019.8847966), [arXiv:1907.01623](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.01623).
- [VERIFIED, primary record] Pfau, J., Liapis, A., Volkmar, G., Yannakakis, G. N., Malaka, R. (2020). "Dungeons & Replicants: Automated Game Balancing via Deep Player Behavior Modeling." IEEE CoG 2020, pp. 431-438. Deep behaviour models of 213 Aion players generate agents that replay encounters; found significant balance differences and class-versus-class results consistent with designer intent; argues heuristic or optimal agents do not represent real players. [DOI 10.1109/CoG47356.2020.9231958](https://doi.org/10.1109/CoG47356.2020.9231958). Journal follow-up: IEEE Transactions on Games 15(2), 217-227, 2023, [DOI 10.1109/TG.2022.3167728](https://doi.org/10.1109/TG.2022.3167728).
- [VERIFIED, primary record] Hernandez, D., Gbadamosi, C. T. T., Goodman, J., Walker, J. A. (2020). "Metagame Autobalancing for Competitive Multiplayer Games." IEEE CoG 2020, pp. 275-282. The designer draws a target graph of how strong each strategy or character type should be against the others; simulation-based optimisation searches game parameters that minimise the distance to that graph; tested on Rock-Paper-Scissors variants and an asymmetric fighting game. Explicitly not "equal win chances for everything". [DOI 10.1109/CoG47356.2020.9231762](https://doi.org/10.1109/CoG47356.2020.9231762), [arXiv:2006.04419](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04419).
- [VERIFIED, primary record] Rupp, F., Eberhardinger, M., Eckert, K. (2023). "Balancing of competitive two-player Game Levels with Reinforcement Learning." IEEE CoG 2023. PCGRL agent rewarded by repeated simulation toward equal win rates, swap-based edits. [DOI 10.1109/CoG57401.2023.10333248](https://doi.org/10.1109/CoG57401.2023.10333248), [arXiv:2306.04429](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04429). Journal version: "Simulation-Driven Balancing of Competitive Game Levels with Reinforcement Learning", IEEE Transactions on Games, [DOI 10.1109/TG.2024.3399536](https://doi.org/10.1109/TG.2024.3399536), [arXiv:2503.18748](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18748) (adds non-equal balance targets and a search-based baseline).

Human survey: automated balancing improved perceived balance in most scenarios, with differences between scenarios. [DOI 10.1109/CoG60054.2024.10645642](https://doi.org/10.1109/CoG60054.2024.10645642), [arXiv:2407.11396](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11396).
- [VERIFIED, primary record] Rupp, F., Eckert, K. (2025). "Level the Level: Balancing Game Levels for Asymmetric Player Archetypes With Reinforcement Learning." FDG 2025. [DOI 10.1145/3723498.3723747](https://doi.org/10.1145/3723498.3723747), [arXiv:2503.24099](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.24099). [arXiv:2408.17180](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.17180).

- [VERIFIED, primary record] Ye, D., Chen, G., Zhang, W., Chen, S., Yuan, B., Liu, B., et al. (2020). "Towards Playing Full MOBA Games with Deep Reinforcement Learning." NeurIPS 2020. LLM agents self-play a small civilisation-style game from a text rulebook; Bayesian optimisation over rule parameters with adaptive sampling (more games for promising candidates); metric: win-rate disparity. No numbers in the abstract, not peer reviewed. [arXiv:2602.06232](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06232).

### Inferences
- [INFERRED, arithmetic] The draft has 600 kits. A full 1v1 round robin is 600 x 599 / 2 = 179,700 pairings; the 3v3 team space is C(600,3) = 35,820,200 compositions per side. Exhaustive testing of teams is not practical; testing the 22 axis levels (3 classes, 5 shapes, 10 effects, 4 deliveries) is.
- [INFERRED] Practical pipeline for a deterministic simulation with existing bots: (1) main-effect screening per axis level with mirrored kits that differ in one level only, which is restricted play in Jaffe's sense; (2) a sampled round robin of kits giving a win matrix; (3) Bradley-Terry or Elo strength plus a counter table (Lin et al.) and Nash averaging (Balduzzi et al.) so that hundreds of near-duplicate kits do not bias the rating; (4) evolutionary or Bayesian search over the per-level numbers toward a target class counter graph (Hernandez et al.), penalising the number of changes (de Mesentier Silva et al. 2019); (5) several bot personas instead of one (de Mesentier Silva 2017, Holmgard 2019), since one policy's weaknesses become false balance signals; (6) a human check, because bots are not players (Pfau et al.) and measured balance is not perceived quality (Rupp et al. 2024).
- [INFERRED] LLM agents are, as of October 2026, shown on turn-based or text-described games only; no peer-reviewed result was found for real-time top-down combat. For this project they are a possible reviewer of patch notes or a slow strategic bot, not a replacement for scripted or learned bots.

### Gaps
- Full texts of Jaffe 2012 and Pfau 2020 could not be parsed; numeric results are not reported here.
- No public description of how Supercell or Riot use bots for balance was found. Tencent's balance tool is asserted in one sentence, without method.
- No 2024 to 2026 paper applying automated balancing to a 3v3 brawler with modular kits was found.

## 7. Crowd control and support effects in short-session mobile games

### Takeaway
In Brawl Stars as patched in 2026, hard stuns sit between 0.5 and 2 seconds and the recent direction is downward, invisibility is a 6-second Super on a single high-rarity assassin, and stacking slows are treated as a bug. No studio publishes a recommended duration; the usable evidence is patch values, taxonomies and the "anti-fun" principle.

### Cited Findings
- [VERIFIED, primary, measured] Brawl Stars stun and slow changes in the February to March 2026 notes: Bull's Stomper slow and stun 2s to 1s; Kit's stun 2s to 1.5s; Buzz's minimum stun 0.5s to 0.6s; Bo's Snare A Bear replaces knockback with a 2-second stun; Squeak's slow "could stack and immobilize players" (fixed). Shield colours are standardised by meaning (white invulnerable on respawn, yellow damage reduction, purple-blue extra health). No reasons are given for the stun changes. Source: [Release Notes February 2026](https://supercell.com/en/games/brawlstars/blog/release-notes/release-notes-february-2026/).
- [VERIFIED, wiki, measured] Frank's Super "stuns enemies for 2 seconds"; his Gadget Buffie slow was cut to 2 seconds from 5. Leon's Super: "Leon becomes invisible for 6 seconds." Sandy's Sweet Dreams gadget stun is 1 second after a reduction on 25/08/21. Sources: [Brawl Stars Wiki, Frank](https://brawlstars.fandom.com/wiki/Frank), [Leon](https://brawlstars.fandom.com/wiki/Leon), [Sandy](https://brawlstars.fandom.com/wiki/Sandy) (read through the wiki API).
- [VERIFIED, primary, measured, paraphrased] April 2026: Leon nerfed (HP 3600 to 3300, speed bonuses no longer stack) as consistently top-performing, to feel like a glass cannon; Pierce's slow 30% to 25% because his Super was "too oppressive"; Ziggy's projectile delay raised 700 to 750 ms to give "more time to dodge"; Najia's second projectile "no longer auto-aims". Source: [Release Notes April 2026](https://supercell.com/en/games/brawlstars/blog/release-notes/release-notes-april-2026/).
- [VERIFIED, wiki on Riot's domain, measured] League separates crowd control by what it removes (movement, attacks, abilities) and by what counters it (Tenacity, cleanses). "Immobilizing" effects are a named subset. Source: [Types of Crowd Control](https://wiki.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/Types_of_Crowd_Control).
- [VERIFIED, secondary, principle] Riot's 2016 stealth rework split stealth into Camouflage (long, strategic, revealed by proximity and by Control Wards) and Invisibility (short, tactical; turrets reveal; a hit makes the target shimmer briefly), stated as adding consistency and allowing power to be returned to stealth champions once counterplay was uniform. Source: [Red Bull, LoL's assassin update](https://www.redbull.com/au-en/lols-assassin-update-adds-danger-to-the-shadows).
- [VERIFIED, repost, opinion] Riot's anti-pattern test for control and stealth effects: a mechanic is a problem when the "anti-fun" for the victim exceeds the user's fun; team auras are the example of "power without gameplay". Source: [Zileas' anti-fun patterns](https://lawofgamedesign.com/2014/02/20/zileas-list-of-game-design-anti-fun-patterns/).
- [VERIFIED, wiki plus secondary, measured] Diminishing returns as a chain-control limiter: WoW's 18-second window with full, half, then reduced or immune (see section 3 sources).
- [VERIFIED, primary, principle, paraphrased] Supercell's Support class definition: heals or buffs allies, low burst, fair HP; Supports are listed as targets for Damage Dealers, Assassins, Marksmen and Tanks. Source: [#MysteryAtTheHub Patch Notes](https://supercell.com/en/games/brawlstars/blog/release-notes/mysteryatthehub-patch-notes/).

### Inferences
- [INFERRED] In a 3v3 match of a few minutes, a 2-second stun is a large share of a typical engagement, and three players can chain control. Brawl Stars keeps hard stuns short and mostly on Supers, gadgets with cooldowns, or conditional triggers (Bull's stun only on already-slowed targets). This is one studio's practice, read from patch values, not a stated rule.
- [INFERRED] Of the draft's ten aura values, stun and invisibility are the two that the evidence singles out as needing structural limits (duration cap, reveal rule, no chaining), and "ally power buff" and "haste" risk the "power without gameplay" pattern if they are passive auras and not visible, timed actions.
- [INFERRED] Heal, cleanse and buffs define a Support role by themselves. If aura alone can make a unit a support, then class (outfit) and aura will sometimes disagree (a "tank" outfit with a heal aura), which needs either a compatible stat envelope or an accepted hybrid label.

### Gaps
- No published Supercell or Riot statement giving recommended stun or invisibility durations for mobile was found. Wild Rift-specific crowd control constraints: not found.
- No study measuring player frustration by control type or duration was found. Vicencio-Moreira et al. and similar HCI work address aiming, not control effects.
- Whether Brawl Stars has a global diminishing-returns or immunity rule after a stun: not found in the notes read.

## 8. Evidence about "fun and immediately understandable" combat for a casual or crypto-native audience

### Takeaway
The strongest evidence is Supercell's own history: the control scheme, not the content, decided whether Brawl Stars survived soft launch, and the final scheme was landscape with two virtual sticks. Peer-reviewed work adds that tutorials only pay off for complex, unconventional mechanics and that aim assistance narrows skill gaps and raises enjoyment for weaker players. Nothing specific to a crypto-native audience was found beyond Axie's own rationale for Origins.

### Cited Findings
- [VERIFIED, secondary, measured] Brawl Stars "started out as a portrait-mode tapper"; "the devs quickly noticed that people enjoyed playing with a joystick on their screen. This inspired them to invert the game and throwing in a second joystick." Frank Keienburg: "We started with a fun game but we weren't sure how to interpret it's success"; "we wanted to make a game that was ready to play five years from now". ### Inferences
- [INFERRED] Button budget in the mobile leader: Brawl Stars exposes move, attack, Super and one Gadget, plus a Hypercharge trigger later in progression, so a new player faces two action buttons. The draft's basic attack plus one signature ability fits that budget.
- [INFERRED] Following Andersen et al., the trait system should be discoverable by play (see a cone, see a burn) and reserve explicit tutorials for the unconventional parts (delivery modes such as echo and return).
- [INFERRED] Following Bateman et al. and Supercell's tap-to-fire practice, an aim-assist default with manual aiming available is the evidence-backed choice for a casual audience; skill expression then lives in manual aim and timing.

## Implications for a trait-derived roster

Status of every rule below: INFERRED design guidance, traced to the verified findings named in brackets. Numbers are starting values to test in simulation, not laws. The draft under review: outfit sets class (3), eyes set shape (5), aura sets effect (10 including none), rarity sets delivery (4) without changing power.

### What the draft already gets right
- One effect per axis and a small authored vocabulary (19 blocks giving 200 abilities). Same structure as Hades slots and Magicka cast modes. [Section 3: Hades Wiki, Wikipedia Magicka]
- Rarity as delivery instead of stats avoids the DeFi Kingdoms and Galaxy Fight Club pattern. [Section 4: DFK docs, GFC whitepaper]

### Where the evidence contests the draft
1. Three classes is too coarse for readability. Use 5 to 7 mechanically defined roles, or keep 3 outfit families and derive a second role label from the kit (range band from shape, support flag from aura), in the style of Dota's graded role vector. Publish the counter relations between roles. [Section 1: Supercell 2023 notes, Blizzard Hero Role Expansion, Riot classes, Dota 2 data feed]
2. "Rarity changes delivery, not power" is not true by construction. Echo, return and persistent zone each add expected damage, area or uptime. Each delivery needs a price on the cost curve, paid in base damage, cooldown or range, so that every kit has the same budget. [Section 5: Schreiber Level 3; Valve on trade-offs; Path of Exile supports raise mana cost]
3. Stun and invisibility should not be ordinary aura values on a short cooldown. [Section 7: Brawl Stars patch values; Riot stealth split; Zileas anti-fun]
4. "None" as an aura must be a real kit, with its whole budget spent on raw numbers, and must be explained to holders as a specialist, not as a missing feature. [Section 4: Aavegotchi two-sided map; Section 5: cost curve]

### Design rules
- R1. Define each role as a stat envelope plus a job and print it on the hero card. Keep an explicit "hybrid" label for outfit and aura combinations that cross roles. [Supercell 2023 class definitions; Pokemon UNITE battle types; Riot Specialist and hybrids]
- R2. Build a deliberate intransitive triangle or wheel between roles and solve the expected pick mix before tuning numbers; compare with bot and player pick rates. [Schreiber Level 9; Supercell published counters; Loot Survivor triangle]
- R3. One modifier per axis, replacement and never accumulation. Same-effect reapplication refreshes duration and does not stack magnitude. Write the full interaction table for the 9 effects (for example cleanse removes burn, slow, poison; heal and poison both apply). [Hades one boon per slot; Path of Exile duplicate rule; Brawl Stars Squeak slow fix; Genshin reaction table; Magicka precedence]
- R4. One keyword, one colour and one particle language per aura effect, identical on every shape. The primary visual element is the true hit area; theme and rarity flourishes are secondary and lower contrast; visual loudness scales with gameplay impact. [Riot VFX style guide; Slay the Spire keywords; Supercell shield colour standard]
- R5. Rarity is specialisation and expressiveness, never budget. Give higher tiers the deliveries with more comprehension complexity (return, echo) and price them to equal budget. Verify with a measurable gate: win rate grouped by rarity tier must stay inside the chosen band in bot matrices and in live data. [Magic New World Order; Aavegotchi trait map; Dota 2 complexity rating; Supercell Buffies statement on raw strength]
- R6. Price blocks bottom-up: single shapes with no effect first, then each effect on one reference shape, then each delivery on one reference kit, then spot-check combinations for super-additive pairs (persistent zone with slow, piercing wave with stun). When unsure, ship weak. [Schreiber Level 3]
- R7. Control caps to start from: hard stun at most 1.0 to 1.5 seconds on a signature ability, slow magnitudes around 25 to 30%, a per-target diminishing rule (second stun within a fixed window halved, third ignored), invisibility at most a few seconds with reveal on dealing or taking damage and on proximity. [Brawl Stars 2026 values: stuns 0.5 to 2 s trending down, Pierce slow 30 to 25%, Leon 6 s on a Super; WoW diminishing returns; Riot Camouflage versus Invisibility; LoL Tenacity table]
- R8. Make support effects active and visible: timed zones or pulses with a clear radius, not passive auras. [Zileas, Power Without Gameplay; Supercell Support definition]
- R9. Balance by tuning the 22 axis levels, never an individual NFT. An NFT's kit identity (shape, effect, delivery, role) stays fixed for holders; numbers per level change in dated patch notes that give reasons. [Supercell release notes format; Riot framework aim of limiting subjective judgment; Axie's continuity promise to holders]
- R10. Publish the full trait-to-kit table and all numbers. Do not hide stats. Put contentious mapping choices to holders before lock. [Galaxy Fight Club as the opaque counter-example; Aavegotchi DAO vote; Pixelmon reveal]
- R11. Guarantee a free or baseline unit with full budget so that owning an NFT changes kit variety and cosmetics, not match odds. [Axie Origins starter Axies; Galaxy Fight Club default fighter]
- R12. Adopt explicit bands. Starting proposal: flag a level above 54% or below 49% win rate in human play, following Riot's published Average band, and use a wider band plus spread across personas for bot matrices. Overpowered if any persona or skill group crosses the top, underpowered only if all are below the floor. [Riot Champion Balance Framework 2019 and 2020 update]
- R13. Simulation loop with the existing deterministic sim and bots: restricted-play screening per level, sampled kit round robin, Bradley-Terry or Elo with a counter table and Nash averaging, parameter search toward the target role graph with a penalty on the number of changes, at least two bot personas, then human playtest for perceived fairness. [Jaffe 2012; Lin 2024; Balduzzi 2018; Hernandez 2020; de Mesentier Silva 2017 and 2019; Pfau 2020; Rupp 2024]
- R14. Controls and onboarding: two action buttons at first contact, landscape dual stick, aim assist by default with manual aim available, mechanics learnable by seeing them, explicit teaching only for delivery modes. [Newsweek and GDC 2019 on Brawl Stars; Bateman 2011; Andersen 2012]
- R15. Scope for first playable: the 19 blocks with distinct silhouettes are comparable in ability variety to a 20 to 40 hero launch roster. Ship fewer effects first if any block cannot be made visually unambiguous. [Section 2 launch rosters; Riot VFX clarity goals; Magic comprehension complexity]

### Flip conditions and the fastest test for each
- If equal-budget tuning cannot bring every delivery tier inside the band in the bot matrix, rarity as delivery is not power-neutral: move delivery to a non-rarity trait and make rarity cosmetic. Test: restricted-play screening of the 4 deliveries on 3 reference kits.
- If the bot Nash mix over roles puts more than half the weight on one outfit class, three classes are degenerate: add the second role axis. Test: round robin of 3 classes x 5 shapes with aura "none".
- If playtesters cannot name an opponent's effect and shape after one exchange, the vocabulary is too large or the VFX too similar: cut effects before adding content. Test: 10-minute blind identification session with 5 outside players.
- If stun or invisibility kits show normal win rates but the highest quit or complaint rates, cap further or move them to a charged ultimate. Test: tag matches by enemy effect and compare early-leave rates.
