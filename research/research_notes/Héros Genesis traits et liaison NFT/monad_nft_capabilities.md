## 1. Monad documentation: EVM compatibility and the differences that matter for NFTs and games

### Takeaway
Monad mainnet is a Fusaka-level EVM (all opcodes, all Ethereum precompiles, plus P256 and staking precompiles) with 300 ms blocks, finality at 2 blocks, a 128 KB contract size limit, EIP-7702 enabled and canonical ERC-4337 EntryPoints deployed. The differences that actually bite a game are operational: gas is charged on the gas LIMIT, `eth_getLogs` is capped at 100 blocks on the default public RPC, old state is not served by every endpoint, and an EIP-7702-delegated account cannot spend its MON balance below 10 MON.

### Inferences
- [INFERRED] Because the gas limit is charged, every game transaction (and every sponsored transaction) should carry a tight, hardcoded gas limit. Wallet defaults that pad the estimate make the user or the paymaster pay for the padding.
- [INFERRED] A purchase priced in native MON is fragile for players whose EOA is EIP-7702-delegated (MetaMask smart account, embedded wallets using 7702): any spend that leaves the account under 10 MON reverts. Pricing in an ERC-20 such as $CHOG avoids the rule, since the rule only concerns the MON balance.
- [INFERRED] `block.timestamp` cannot order events inside one second (3 to 4 blocks share a timestamp). On-chain game logic that needs ordering or cooldown precision below one second must use `block.number`.
- [INFERRED] The comment in the Genesis source ("Monad's RPC rejects any `eth_getLogs` spanning more than 100 blocks") describes the QuickNode and Monad Foundation endpoints, not the protocol. Goldsky Edge (`rpc2`) served 10,001 blocks in one call in this session. A log replay of the collection through plain RPC is therefore feasible on `rpc2` (about 1,010 calls for the 10.1M blocks since deployment) but not on `rpc.monad.xyz` (about 101,000 calls).
- [INFERRED] Crediting a purchase is safe at `finalized` (about 600 ms, 2 blocks behind `latest` as measured). Showing optimistic UI at `latest` is what Monad itself recommends for ordinary apps.
- [INFERRED] The viem chain definition still declares `blockTime: 400` for Monad while the chain runs at about 300 ms since MIP-12 ("block times reduced from 400 ms to 300 ms", per the Monad release notes), so polling intervals derived from viem defaults are slightly conservative. Source for the value: [viem monad.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wevm/viem/main/src/chains/definitions/monad.ts)

### Gaps
- [UNKNOWN] No published retention guarantee for historical state on any public endpoint. The 35-day lookback observed on `rpc1`, `rpc2` and `rpc-mainnet.monadinfra.com` is a measurement, not a commitment; only `rpc2` and `rpc-mainnet.monadinfra.com` are documented as supporting historical state.
- [UNKNOWN] The exact maximum `eth_getLogs` range of `rpc2.monad.xyz`: 10,001 blocks worked, while 100,001 blocks was rejected with `{"code":-32012,"message":"getLogs request exceeded max allowed range"}`, so the limit lies between the two. For `rpc3.monad.xyz` the limit lies between 102 and 1,000 blocks.
- [UNKNOWN] Which of the three version statements in the docs is stale was resolved by the RPC (0.16.4), but the docs do not state the mainnet activation date of MIP-8 other than through the timestamp in `networks.json`.

- [INFERRED] At the minimum base fee, sponsoring is cheap: a 200,000-gas purchase costs the sponsor about 0.02 MON (Monad's own figure), so 10,000 sponsored purchases cost about 200 MON at the floor price.
- [INFERRED] Token-bound accounts (Tokenbound V3) can also be driven through ERC-4337 because they point at EntryPoint v0.6, which Pimlico serves with both bundler and paymaster on chain 143.
